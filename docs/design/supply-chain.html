Supply chain records

The supply chain records of repoctl: the standards, the threats and the choice, then the content, the files and the verification chain of each record. The chapter ends with the options that the design does not use and the regulation.

  • For each build, repoctl writes one SLSA provenance record (an in-toto Statement) and one CycloneDX 1.6 SBOM. Each target repository also has one SBOM.
  • No extra keys and no second trust system. The records are files in the snapshot. The RepoManifest lists their sha256, and the signed repomd.xml covers the RepoManifest.
  • The build captures the facts that only the build can see: the mock buildroot lock, the source archives and the run facts.
  • Publish writes the records in step 3, after the sign step. rpmsign changes the sha256 of each RPM file, and the records name the signed files.
  • The set builds have SLSA Build L1. L2 needs a hosted build platform, and each admin builds on an own host.
  • The design does not use cosign, keyless Sigstore, OCI referrers, in-toto layouts or hosted builds. The dnf clients do not use them.

Why records

repoctl signs each RPM and each repomd.xml. A signature tells a client two facts: the file did not change, and a trusted key signed it. It does not tell what is in the file, or how the file was made. Supply chain records give these facts in a standard form that other tools can read.

A good record answers three questions:

QuestionFactsRecord
What is in it?The components, their versions, licenses and hashes.SBOM (software bill of materials)
How was it made?The source commit, the build platform, the parameters and the build dependencies.Provenance
Who says so, and can I trust it?A signature over the record, and a way to find the record.The attestation envelope and its distribution

repoctl already knows most of these facts: the RepoManifest (model.html#manifest) and the snapshot annotations (publish.html#format) hold them. The records give the same facts, and a few more, in formats that scanners and auditors read.

The four standards

The four standards are not alternatives. Each standard is one layer, and a full attestation uses one item from each layer.

SBOM: SPDX and CycloneDX

An SBOM lists the components of a piece of software. Each component has a name, a version, a package URL (purl), hashes, a license and its relations to other components. Two formats are in common use (comparison):

  • SPDX (Linux Foundation). SPDX 3.0 is a new model with profiles (software, build, security, licensing).
  • CycloneDX (OWASP). Version 1.7 came in October 2025. CycloneDX also carries VEX (vulnerability exploitability) and a crypto bill of materials.

For an RPM, an SBOM gives the SRPM, the binary RPMs, the upstream source archives, the bundled libraries (Provides: bundled(…)) and the licenses. A vulnerability scanner can then match the upstream versions with a vulnerability database.

SLSA provenance

SLSA (Supply-chain Levels for Software Artifacts, OpenSSF) has tracks with levels. The build track has levels L0 to L3, and version 1.2 (November 2025) adds a source track. The build track asks for provenance: a record of the build platform, the build type, the parameters, the resolved dependencies and the run.

LevelRequirementProtects against
L0Nothing.Nothing.
L1Provenance exists and the consumer can get it. It can be incomplete and unsigned.Mistakes, for example a build from a commit that is not on the remote.
L2A hosted build platform generates and signs the provenance. Hosted means "not on an individual's workstation".Tampering after the build.
L3A hardened platform: builds are isolated, and user-defined build steps cannot read the signing key of the provenance.Tampering during the build.

in-toto attestations

in-toto (CNCF) gives the common frame:

  • A Statement binds a predicate (the facts, for example SLSA provenance or an SBOM) to one or more subjects (artifacts, by name and digest).
  • A DSSE envelope can sign the Statement. A bundle is a JSON Lines file with many envelopes.
  • A layout is a signed policy that names the steps of a supply chain and the humans that can do each step.

Sigstore and cosign

Sigstore (OpenSSF) signs artifacts and attestations:

  • cosign is its client. It signs with a key pair, a KMS key or a hardware key (PIV, for example on a YubiKey).
  • In keyless mode, Fulcio gives a short-lived certificate for an OIDC identity, for example a GitHub workflow or an e-mail address.
  • Rekor is a public transparency log: each signature gets a log entry that anyone can audit.
  • For OCI artifacts, cosign stores signatures and attestations in the registry next to the artifact, with the OCI referrers API or a tag scheme.

What the EL clients verify

rpm and dnf on EL9 and EL10 verify OpenPGP signatures only: the RPM signature and repomd.xml.asc (signing facts). They do not read SBOMs, provenance or Sigstore signatures. Thus no record changes what a client installs. The records are for humans and tools that ask questions about the packages.

Threats and value

The value of the records is in T3 to T5 and T8, not in tamper protection.

#Threat or questionControlPart of the records
T1An RPM changes after the publish (GHCR, mirror, HTTP server).RPM signature, repomd.xml.asc, mirror job verify.None. The OpenPGP signatures stop this.
T2The served tree is old or comes from another snapshot.repoctl-snapshot.json, status served:, the signed RepoManifest names the publish.None.

Why records, but no extra signatures

T1, T2 and T7 are about trust in files. The OpenPGP chain covers T1 and T2, and the clients use only that chain. T3, T4, T5 and T8 are about knowledge. They need good data in a standard format. They do not need a second signature system.

The choice

repoctl writes two predicates and no envelope.

Records

repoctl captures the facts during the build and writes the records during the publish.

Where the records come from

Rule: records use the digests of the signed files

A client downloads and scans the signed file. Thus each subject and each SBOM hash is the sha256 of the signed file, and repoctl writes the records in step 3, after step 2.

rpmsign --resign replaces the signature header of each new RPM. The sha256 of the file changes, but the header digest (SHA256HEADER), the payload digest and SIGMD5 stay. Test V24 confirms the digest facts.

Capture at build time

The build step captures the facts that exist only during the build:

  • Buildroot lock. The mock plugin buildroot_lock is on for each job (config_opts["plugin_conf"]["buildroot_lock_enable"] = True). The plugin ships with mock 5.7 and later; Fedora has mock 6. It writes buildroot_lock.json to the result directory. The file lists each RPM in the buildroot with name, epoch, version, release, arch, license, SIGMD5, the signer key id and the URL. It also gives the digest of the bootstrap image. The file is the buildrootFile of the RepoManifest (model.html#manifest).
  • Source archives. The SRPM holds each source archive and each patch. repoctl takes the sha256 of each file from the SRPM, and the URL of each Source line from the spec. It already parses the spec in the target chroot for the graph.
  • Run facts. The start and the end of the node build, the mock version, the repoctl version, the user and the host. The journal has them (node.state).
  • Set dependencies. Each buildroot RPM that comes from the set (from staging or from latest) gets the purl of the set. repoctl finds it by NEVRA in the staging or in the RepoManifest.

The draft stays in the staging next to the RPMs (build.html#staging). It is not signed and not shared. The facts are lost when the build ends, so a build without them cannot get its provenance later.

Build type mock-rpm/v1. This paragraph documents the build type, because SLSA asks for a documented build type. One node build checks out commit of source. It makes the SRPM and the RPMs with mock in the config mockConfig of target, with rpmautospec. The extra repositories are the staging of the run and the local copy of latest.

externalParameters are the values a human controls through the set. internalParameters are the values of the admin host. Two builds with equal externalParameters and equal resolvedDependencies are the same build.

Builder admin-host. The builder is the repoctl process of one admin on one host. The admin is the trust boundary: the same human builds, signs and publishes. This is SLSA Build L1. The record is unsigned in itself (L1 allows it), and the repository signature covers it.

The buildroot digests are SIGMD5 values from the buildroot lock, marked as such. MD5 is weak, but the record uses it to identify a package, not to trust it. The rpm signature of each buildroot package (the signer annotation) gives the trust. When test V23 passes, each entry also gets the SHA256HEADER of the package.

SBOM record

repoctl writes one CycloneDX 1.6 SBOM for each build and one SBOM for the repository of each target. The build SBOM follows the Red Hat CycloneDX guidance for RPMs:

  • metadata.component is the SRPM, with the purl pkg:rpm/gsi-hpc/ucx@1.22.1-1.el9?arch=src&distro=el9.
  • pedigree.ancestors gives the upstream: pkg:github/openucx/ucx@v1.22.1, with the URL and the sha256 of the source archive. A source without a known upstream type gets pkg:generic with download_url and checksum.
  • components[] lists each binary and debuginfo RPM with its purl (?arch=x86_64&distro=el9). Each one has the sha256 of the signed file and the license from the RPM header.
  • Each Provides: bundled(NAME) = VERSION of an RPM gives a component of type library under that RPM.
  • dependencies[] gives the requires that resolve to RPMs of the set. Requires on EL packages stay as properties, because the client resolves them from the EL repositories.
  • The supplier and the purl namespace (gsi-hpc) come from the owner in store.oci.

The repository SBOM has the snapshot as its metadata.component (pkg:oci/slurm-26.05-el9@sha256:9d07…?repository_url=ghcr.io/gsi-hpc/slurm-repo). It lists the build SBOMs of all builds in the snapshot as components. A scanner then needs one URL per target.

The License tag of a spec in EL9 style is not always an SPDX expression. repoctl copies an SPDX expression into licenses[].expression, and any other value into licenses[].license.name. The doctor check sbom.license lists the specs of the set without an SPDX expression. It is a warning, not a gate.

Files and the RepoManifest

The records are files of the binary repository (slurm/), next to the buildroot files. dnf reads only the files that repomd.xml lists as repodata, so it ignores them. Each file is one blob in the snapshot, as each RPM (snapshot format).

slurm/
  Packages/u/ucx-1.22.1-1.el9.x86_64.rpm
  repodata/repomd.xml, repomd.xml.asc, …-repoctl.json  (RepoManifest)
  buildroot/ucx-1.22.1-1.el9.json                   (buildroot_lock.json)
  records/ucx-1.22.1-1.el9/provenance.json
  records/ucx-1.22.1-1.el9/sbom.cdx.json
  records/repository.cdx.json                       (one per snapshot)

Each build entry of the RepoManifest has the field records. The manifest also has one top-level records field for the repository SBOM (model.html#manifest):

EventRecords
publishNew records for each new build. The records of the base stay. A new repository SBOM.
repo resignNew records for each re-signed build: new subject digests and SBOM hashes. All other fields stay, also the build definition. A new repository SBOM.

Verification chain

A reader can trust a record as much as an RPM. The chain starts at the key file of the release package, the same key file that the clients have.

Publish step 4 (verify) and step 5 (verify-el) check each record sha256 against the RepoManifest and each subject against its file. A failure gives records.mismatch (exit 10), and repoctl keeps the tree.

The mirror job does the same check with jq and sha256sum after its gpgv check (publish.html#mirror). It never serves a tree with a bad record. On the story publish:

$ repoctl repo verify --target el9
✓ el9: 208 RPMs in 3 repositories, signers d.klein (158) hpc-repo (50), checksums ok, 3 repomd.xml.asc ok
✓ el9 records: provenance and SBOM equal to the RepoManifest, subjects equal to the files
! el9 records: 48 RPMs of p-import-20260828 have an SBOM and no provenance (adopted)

The records are not in a DSSE envelope. Thus slsa-verifier and cosign verify-attestation do not accept them (test V27). repoctl repo verify and the chain above are the verifier.

The read commands have read-only MCP tools, repoctl_repo_provenance and repoctl_repo_sbom (agents.html#mcp-tools). A skill can then answer T3 and T4 for an admin: "which builds used gcc 11.5.0-5?". The problem codes are records.mismatch and records.missing (contract.html#problems). The stored files keep their own standard schemas; repoctl does not wrap them.

Regulation

From 11 December 2027, the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA, Regulation 2024/2847) asks the manufacturer of a product with digital elements for an SBOM. The CRA names no format. The German guideline BSI TR-03183-2 v2.1 names CycloneDX 1.6 and later or SPDX 3.0.1 and later. The SBOM must have the hashes, the licenses and the dependencies of each shipped component.

The CRA applies to products that a manufacturer places on the EU market. The slurm repositories serve the clusters of the operator, so the CRA probably does not apply. This is not a legal check: the GSI legal or IT security team must confirm it (open questions).

The records do not depend on the answer. CycloneDX 1.6 with hashes and licenses keeps the option open at no extra cost.
